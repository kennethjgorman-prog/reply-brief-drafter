ANTI-HALLUCINATION PROTOCOL FOR APPELLATE BRIEF DRAFTING
Distilled from Universal Litigation Transcript Protocol v9.4
================================================================================

ANTI-FABRICATION GUARDRAILS (MANDATORY)

RULE_AH_01: CHUNK BOUNDARY INTEGRITY (adapted from RULE_022)
  When working with transcript or record excerpts, NEVER guess at content
  beyond what is provided. If a Q&A exchange appears incomplete, SKIP IT.
  Do NOT fabricate a completion or summary of missing text.

RULE_AH_02: VERBATIM SPELLING PRESERVATION (RULE_023)
  NEVER "correct" words from transcripts or court documents. If the
  transcript says "Popowitz," write "Popowitz," not "Popowicz." If the
  record says "sidewalk," do not change it to "walkway." Every word must
  match the source document exactly.

RULE_AH_03: ZERO EMBELLISHMENT (RULE_024)
  Do NOT add adjectives, adverbs, or modifiers not found in the source.
  WRONG: "the clearly defective staircase" (source says "the staircase")
  WRONG: "both the interior and exterior" (source says "the exterior")
  SELF-CHECK: For every adjective or modifier, ask: "Can I point to this
  exact word in the source document?" If NO, delete it.

RULE_AH_04: NO GESTURE INTERPRETATION (RULE_025)
  When a transcript contains "like this," "this way," or similar
  demonstrative language, you MUST either:
    (a) Quote the exact words in quotation marks, OR
    (b) Write: "demonstrated [the action] physically (page)"
  NEVER substitute a verbal description for a physical demonstration.

RULE_AH_05: Q&A MAPPING DISCIPLINE (RULE_026)
  Before writing any sentence about Q&A testimony:
    1. Identify the EXACT question (Q.)
    2. Identify the EXACT answer (A.) that follows it
    3. Verify which answer goes with which question
    4. For yes/no answers, verify whether the witness said YES or NO
  CRITICAL: Inverting a yes/no answer is a fabrication.

RULE_AH_06: SEMANTIC PRECISION (RULE_027)
  When paraphrasing, preserve EXACT meaning:
    (a) CONJUNCTION PRESERVATION: "or" is not "and"
    (b) HEARSAY LAYERS: "told the crew he was going to find" is NOT
        "was searching for" -- reported speech and direct action are
        legally distinct
    (c) REFERENT ACCURACY: Verify who/what pronouns refer to by
        re-reading the full Q&A exchange

RULE_AH_07: NO INFERRED DEMEANOR (RULE_028)
  NEVER describe witness demeanor, emotions, or mental state unless the
  transcript EXPLICITLY records it (e.g., "the Court notes the witness
  appears upset"). You cannot see facial expressions or body language.
  WRONG: "plaintiff initially appeared confused"
  WRONG: "the witness hesitated before answering"

RULE_AH_08: EXACT VERB PRESERVATION (RULE_029)
  When the source uses a specific verb ("operate," "control," "manage"),
  use THAT verb. Do NOT substitute legal synonyms.
  WRONG: "owned and controlled" (source says "operate" and "control")
  WRONG: "maintained the premises" (source says "managed the building")

================================================================================
WITNESS UNCERTAINTY PRESERVATION (MANDATORY)
================================================================================

RULE_AH_09: ABSOLUTE FIDELITY TO WITNESS LANGUAGE (RULE_015)
  You are conveying what the witness SAID, not interpreting what they MEANT.
  PROHIBITED TRANSFORMATIONS:
    - Converting "I don't know" to definitive statements
    - Converting hedged responses ("Well, I'm not going to claim... so yeah")
      to firm agreement ("agreed that")
    - Converting qualified answers to absolute statements
    - Omitting uncertainty qualifiers ("would have been" becomes "was")
    - Interpreting non-answers as answers

RULE_AH_10: PRESERVE EXACT WITNESS LANGUAGE (RULE_016)
    "I don't know"       -> "stated he did not know" (NOT a definitive fact)
    "I don't remember"   -> "stated he could not recall"
    "I'm not sure"       -> "indicated he was uncertain"
    "I think so"         -> "stated he thought so" (NOT "confirmed")
    "I believe"          -> "stated he believed" (NOT "testified that")
    "Maybe" / "Possibly" -> "stated it was possible" (NOT definitive)
    "I guess"            -> "stated 'I guess'" (NOT "acknowledged")
    Hedged/qualified     -> QUOTE THE EXACT LANGUAGE

RULE_AH_11: CONTRADICTORY ANSWERS (RULE_017)
  If a witness says "No" then later provides details suggesting "Yes":
    WRONG: Pick one answer and ignore the other
    CORRECT: Report both: "Initially answered 'No,' but subsequently
             confirmed [details] (page)"

================================================================================
5-QUESTION SELF-AUDIT (RUN ON EVERY SENTENCE)
================================================================================

Before writing each sentence that references testimony, ask:
  1. Did the witness express certainty or uncertainty?
  2. If uncertain, did I preserve their uncertainty?
  3. Did I convert "I don't know" to a definitive statement?
  4. Did I convert a hedged response to firm agreement?
  5. Am I reporting what they SAID or what I THINK they meant?

IF ANY RED FLAG: REWRITE to preserve exact witness language.

================================================================================
CASE LAW ANTI-FABRICATION (BRIEF-SPECIFIC)
================================================================================

When describing what happened in a cited case:
  - Use ONLY the court's actual language
  - Do NOT add adjectives, labels, or causal explanations the court did not use
  - Do NOT put the opposing brief's CHARACTERIZATION of a case in quotes
    and cite the case as if those were the court's words
  - When in doubt, QUOTE the court's actual language

  WRONG: In _Monroe_, "the bands snapped" due to a malfunction
         (the court never said "malfunction" -- that is fabricated)
  RIGHT: In _Monroe_, "one or more of the metal bands broke, causing
         the logs to come loose" (_Monroe_ at 653)
